BEST REFERENCE WEB SITES
                                     
   EACH YEAR IT IS increasingly difficult to narrow down the list of Best
   Reference Web Sites. Mindful of the subjects and fast pace librarians
   face at the reference desk, we chose authoritative sites most suitable
   for quick reference.
College Rankings
http://www.library.uiuc.edu/edx/rankings.htm   
   The site assembles without endorsement the myriad efforts to rank
   colleges and graduate programs. The left-hand toolbar links to
   undergraduate, graduate, business, law, and international programs.
   Creator Daniel Burgard, a librarian at the University of Illinois,
   also provides a guide to print sources and a bibliography.
411Stocks
http://www.411stocks.com
   This site provides quick one-page access to the most requested company
   financial data (profit & loss statements, financial ratios, etc.) and
   stock prices. While perfect for those looking for info prior to a job
   interview, marketers, students, and economists, it is less useful for
   investment advice, as mutual funds aren't included.
The Gallup Organization
http://www.gallup.com 
   Gallup, a leading pollster since 1935, here shares a wealth of
   information from opinion polls, special reports, trends, and social
   audits. With a recent redesign of the site, polls in the news are
   prominently highlighted. Click on the Gallup Poll tab to retrieve the
   latest release or locate polls dating back to January 1997. Techniques
   are explained in the Poll's FAQs.
How Stuff Works
http://www.howstuffworks.com
   Created by Marshall Brain, a former computer science professor at
   North Carolina State University, this site offers clear explanations
   about the operation of a wide range of things: car engines, VCRs, web
   pages, bread, and much more. Click on the Power Panel for the table of
   contents and search index.
Information Please
http://www.infoplease.com
   Information Please almanacs, popular since 1947 (and growing out of
   the radio quiz show, 1938-52), have moved to the web. Search all or
   individual databases (three almanacs, plus biography, dictionary, and
   online encyclopedia databases), or browse through categorized topics.
   "Facts behind the news" adds a web-based feature.
The Internet Movie Database
http://www.imdb.com
   Now part of the Amazon.com group, this is the largest and best of the
   movie web sites, providing comprehensive information about some
   170,000 titles. Search by title or name from the homepage, or click
   further to search by character, keywords, or cast members. Also
   available are news and box office statistics.
MedicineNet
http://www.medicinenet.com
   For information on diseases, procedures, and pharmaceuticals--three
   main links-this site offers a "forum;' with a main article, plus links
   to related information. The material, contributed by physicians, is
   thorough yet accessible--though of course it cannot substitute for a
   doctor's care. NAICS (North American Industry Classification System)
   http://www.census.gov/epcd/www/naics.html
  
   NAICS replaces the Standard Industrial Classification Manual (SIC), a
   reference tool consulted since the 1930s by anyone using economic
   census or other industrial data. The newly revised,
   production-oriented classification system, with 358 new industries, is
   now standard for North America, though not completely phased-in. The
   web site offers valuable correspondence tables between the two
   systems.
The Nobel Prizes
http://www.nobel.se/prize/
   This official web site of the Nobel Foundation offers a searchable
   database of prizes and laureates that can be searched by prize
   category, or by name, nationality, keyword, or birth or death year.
   For recent winners, there's a lengthy biography, plus a bibliography
   and press coverage.
OneLook Dictionaries
http://www.onelook.com
   OneLook offers access to over 460 dictionaries, general and
   specialized (e.g., art, medical, foreign language). The default search
   is for Special Subjects; if you wish to search the entire database,
   the process will be slow. You can also specify and search specific
   dictionaries.
Online Literary Criticism
http://www.ipl.org/ref/litcrit
   The Internet Public Library offers the best starting point for finding
   criticism of American and British literary works, plus authors'
   biographical information. Search by an author's last name, a work's
   title, or literary period. Some citations refer to the full text of
   articles found in Project Muse (restricted access) or Northern Light
   (retrieval cost).
ParkNet
http://www.nps.gov
   Click on the small Visit Your Parks link for detailed maps and general
   information on national parks, our most visited tourist attraction.
   You can search for parks by name, theme, and state. Individual sites
   vary in quality. Click on Info Zone from the homepage for general
   information on the National Park Service.
Policy. com
http://www.policy.com
   Policy.com's Issues Library provides solid, nonpartisan information on
   topics ranging from agriculture to welfare and housing. Each Monday
   the site presents a new Issue of the Week link to a range of reports
   and resources from think tanks, government agencies, and more. The
   weekly archive dates back to August 1996.
Project Vote Smart
http://www.vote-smart.org
   This is the best site for nonpartisan information-biographies,
   campaign finances, issue stands, and more--about state and federal
   candidates and elected officials. There is also material on elections
   and governmental units. U.S. Consumer Gateway http://www.consumer.gov 
  
   Over 40 federal agencies participate in this site, providing
   authoritative consumer information ranging from health issues to
   product safety. Each of the ten main categories has a rich set of
   links, including useful Scam Alerts. The entire site is searchable.
