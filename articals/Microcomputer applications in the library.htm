LIBRARY SOFTWARE REVIEW; SEPTEMBER 1998

MICROCOMPUTER APPLICATIONS IN THE LIBRARY

Info Trac SearchBank

This article describes the four phases the University of San Francisco underwent in moving from Info Trac CD to the character cell, telnet version to the Web interface. It illustrates the steps taken to provide students with full-text remote access to academic databases. Methods of use are compared, and search techniques are illustrated in detail. The databases are briefly described, content coverage and article selection guidelines are discussed, Web system requirements are outlined, and customer support options are listed This is part of an ongoing effort to discuss and evaluate journal databases for community colleges and universities.

Introduction

Community college and university libraries alike subscribe to a wide variety of library databases. Information Access Company (IAC), located in Medford, Massachusetts, provides access to popular magazines, scholarly journals, newsletters, information pamphlets, newspapers, investment reports, newswire releases, and reference books. A variety of platforms are available. This article will document the transition we underwent at the University of San Francisco (USF) Central Valley Regional Library as we moved from CD-ROM disks and telnet-based remote access to Web connections and IP/Remote Patron Authentication Service. It will compare the types of access and explain the search strategies used by our students in Sacramento, California. We find that the majority of students are Internet savvy. Those who aren't come into the library to learn and soon purchase a computer at home or upgrade to one with an Internet connection. Students want full-text access to on-line databases and expect journal articles to be accurate, timely, and easy to retrieve. IAC's InfoTrac SearchBank databases fulfill all three requirements.

Four Phases

Phase One: Info Trac CD

Initially, the Central Valley Regional Library in Sacramento purchased InfoTrac on CD. Students researched topics on a single stand-alone computer that was connected to a CD tower, and disks were updated monthly. Only extended citations were available, but the character-cell interface was easy to use, and maintenance was low. The main campus provided full-text articles for those citations that referenced journals owned by Gleeson Library in San Francisco. We still maintain this platform as a backup method for research. For those librarians interested in stand-alone or networked CD stations, text reference databases are now available on a Windows 95 interface that is more intuitive and user friendly.

Phase Two: Telnetting to InfoTrac on a DOS-Based Computer

In the second phase, we set up a DOS computer, and used a dial-up connection to telnet to an 800 number at the main campus to connect to InfoTrac's character-cell databases. In this version, several display formats were available, including citations, extended citations, abstracts, and full text. When full text wasn't available, regional students requested full-text articles from the main library in San Francisco, either by fax or by phone, and copies arrived at their designated location in about one week. In comparison, main campus students at Gleeson Library located full-text articles in paper journals or newspapers owned by the library and made copies.

Phase Three: Telnetting to InfoTrac on a Windows 3.1 Computer

In the third phase, the Central Valley Regional Library purchased a Windows 3.1 computer and installed USF Point-to-Point Protocol Interact software. WinQVT telnet software was keymapped to conform with the character-cell commands that displayed on our ProQuest, Silver Platter, and InfoTrac character-cell interface. This software was handed out on a floppy disk to students, faculty members, or staff who requested it. Although the keymapping did not work well with all of the vendor databases, it worked well with InfoTrac SearchBank. SearchBank was a popular option because it covered a variety of disciplines and provided some full text. The drawback was that, by this time, most students were used to working with a mouse on Windows-based and Web interfaces.

Example of a Search via WinQVT Telnet

Students using WinQVT telnet software went through the following process:

• Install the WinQVT software onto their personal computer or use a library computer--either the stand-alone CD station or one of the PCs (initially, the regional libraries had DOS and Windows 3.1 workstations for telnetting in addition to the stand-alone CD station).

• Connect to the CD-ROM databases at Gleeson Library in San Francisco from the Regional Services Web page. A link was provided to connect students to the main menu.

• Enter a login at the prompt and select a VT100 terminal type.

• Confirm that choice.

• Select Reference Resources from a menu. Other choices were available, such as the library catalog via telnet, other library catalogs, and other resources.

• Select InfoTrac SearchBank/LegalTrac.

• Enter a name and university ID (currently, a PIN number is also required for those who still use telnet access).

• Choose an available database from Table 1 and read the information displayed below the options (use the arrow keys to highlight the database and press "enter").

Students who telnetted to the main campus library from home were able to get more full-text options than those using the local CD station. In either case, Expanded Academic ASAP was the most popular database used by Central Valley Regional Library students. Business Index ASAP was the next most popular.

Two methods of searching are available to students using InfoTrac: (1) EasyTrac (known as Easy Search in the CD version), which includes two options, Subject Guide and Key Word, and (2) PowerTrac. EasyTrac is designed for basic research, PowerTrac for advanced research. Subject Guide allows you to browse listings of subjects, people, companies, locations, and events that contain words you enter. Key Word Search lets you search for articles by title, author, words in abstracts, or subject combination.

By pressing "%," students can select PowerTrac. This option allows them more control over their search.

PowerTrac incorporates set theory and field searching. Set theory displays the number of results for the first search as the set R1, the results of the second search as the set R2, and so on. Results can easily be combined by adding the set numbers; for example, R1 and R2 and R7. Sets can also be combined with search terms (for example, R3 and inflation). Field searching allows the student to search for information within a given field. Pressing the front slash (/) displays available fields within the database. The searcher simply highlights the desired field (using the keyboard arrow keys), presses "enter" to select the field, types in a search term, and presses "S" to begin the search.

Boolean operators, proximity operators, and range operators can be included with search terms. Boolean operators consist of AND, OR, NOT between words or phrases. Proximity operators, Wn and Nn, can be used to indicate that two terms had to be within a certain number of words of each other. Range operators, SINCE, AFTER, and BEFORE, can be used to specify date ranges.

To create more complex searches, parentheses can be used to nest key word entries that include any of the logical operators listed above. The operation within the parentheses is performed first. Then, the result is combined with the rest of the query.

For example, query Clinton or Gore, and Internet finds either Clinton or Gore first. Then, it combines the results with database entries that include the word Internet (Figure 1). Notice that results can be narrowed, or you can explore similar topics (Figure 2).

The searcher selects Display or presses "enter" to view; citations appear, one under another with available formats listed below. Formats range from extended citation to abstract to text to abstract and text, with many records including full text. Figure 3 illustrates full text available. Symbols, such as +, -, brackets, slash marks, and letters are used to go to the next and previous records, get help, start over, return to review mode, print and mark text, display, search, and review records. The backslash (\) is used to go back one screen at a time and to exit PowerTrac.

One of the perks of using SearchBank in any format (character cell, Windows 95, InfoTrac Web, or Z39.50 client interface) is that you can link records to the library's holdings. An asterisk appears next to the citation to indicate that the journal is owned by the participating library. To obtain information about the location of the periodical, the range of dates covered by the available issues, the format (microfilm, bound volumes, etc.), and any notes (such as whether the periodical is out for binding), the searcher simply presses the asterisk symbol on the computer keyboard. Librarians have the choice of including whatever information they want students, faculty, and staff to see. In our case, the location of journals owned by USF simply indicates PERIODICALS DEPT. Periodicals are shelved alphabetically by title.

To see the full text of record number 3, the searcher presses "enter." Figure 4 illustrates the full text of the third article of 170 retrieved records.

PowerTrac is also relatively simple to use, even in the character-cell format. Figure 5 illustrates a combination of searches. Notice that the number of retrieved records has been narrowed twice, once by combining results 1 and 2, the second time by combining results 3 and 4. Figure 6 shows the results from search 5.

At the present time, character-based searching is stIll available for those in our user population who want to use that format. However, Web-based full-text databases are preferred by the student body because they are easy to use and are available from any location twenty-four hours a day, seven days a week. For students at USF's regional sites who are taking condensed six-week or eight-week baccalaureate and master's courses over a period of twenty-two to twenty-six months, remote access to full text is particularly important. With research papers due every week or two, students are on a tight schedule to read scholarly materials, write research papers, and make presentations.

In the Sacramento area, community colleges and universities are increasingly offering full-text databases via the Web, as well as via CD stations. With recent grant money in their library budgets, American River College, Sacramento City College, and Consumnes River College have pooled resources to offer Los Rios Community College District students a variety of full-text databases, including EBSCOhost, the Sacramento Bee, and NewsBank InfoWeb--along with CD versions of The New York TImes, the Wall Street Journal, and Business NewsBank. Competing universities in the area, such as National University, the University of Phoenix, and Golden Gate University, also offer full-text databases to their students.

Phase Four: Using Info Trac SearchBank on the Web from a Windows 95 Computer

The fourth phase involved getting Windows 95 computers for the Central Valley Regional Library and connecting to InfoTrac SearchBank via the Web. USF librarians began to evaluate several on-line Web-based periodical databases in 1997 and determined that more full-text options were needed, preferably via the Web (to view the list of InfoTrac SearchBank's Web-based library databases, go to the following URL: http://www.iacnet.com/library/html//search bank.html).

The Web version of InfoTrac SearchBank was first made available to students through an IP authentication process set up in USF libraries. Librarians and' students tested the options, and it was decided to offer remote access. In May 1998, access was shifted to remote patron authentication. Students, faculty, and staff individually enter a name, university ID, and PIN number on a Web page form from any location. The unique identifiers were those listed in our Innovative Interfaces Inc. patron database. After the information is entered, SearchBank software authenticates the user against the library patron database or file. If access is granted, the InfoTrac SearchBank page displays.

There are two ways for librarians to set up remote patron authentication. They can purchase InfoTrac SearchBank RPAS (Remote Patron Authentication Service) software from Information Access or they can check with their automated library system vendor to see if it offers patron authentication software for remote databases. In our case, USF purchased the software from Innovative Interfaces Inc. so that we could run several electronic databases from a single patron database.

It is important to note that you may experience glitches in conforming to different vendor software. Initially, programmers from Innovative Interfaces Inc. had to work with librarians at USF to eliminate a problem that occurred in displaying extended citations, abstracts, and full-text articles.

Using PowerTrac

For more complex searches, the searcher can click the PowerTrac icon on the bottom left of the EasyTrac Web search screen (see Figure 10 for the EasyTrac search screen). The PowerTrac search screen is shown in Figure 24.

On the PowerTrac search screen, you can click in the entry box to enter a search expression. PowerTrac allows you to limit your search within a variety of fields, called search expressions. Each database offers a different subset of options:

• Journal Name (JN): The name of the journal or publication

• Author (AU): The author of the article or document

• Title (TI): The title of the article or document

• Date (DA): The publication date (can be searched with range operators, such as AFTER, BEFORE, TO, and SINCE)

• Text Word (TX): Words found in the body of the article or document

• Abstract (AB): Words included in the abstract of the article or document

• Key Word (KE): Words in the title, name of the author, subject, people, companies, products, locations, events, and so on

• Subject (SU): Words in the subjects describing the article or document

• Record Number (RN): The unique letter/number combination that identifies the article or document

• Article Content (AC): Full-text articles or documents only

• Source (SO): Allows you to search for specific sources, such as reference books

• Refereed (RE): Peer-reviewed articles or documents

• SIC Code (SI): Allows you to search for Standard Industrial Classification code

• Company (CO): Lets you search by company name

• Employees (EM): Allows you to search by number of employees (can be used with range operators)

• Sales (SA): Lets you search for company reports by annual revenue in U.S. dollars

• Industry (IN): Lets you search by industry

• Industry Code (ID): Lets you search by the four-digit SIC code that designates an industry discussed in an investment report

• Report Type (RT): Lets you search for a company report (cr) or an industry report (it) by typing "cr" or "ir" after selecting the index abbreviation.

If you prefer, you can enter the two-letter abbreviation followed by your search term. To browse the index of search expressions, you can type the two-letter abbreviation followed by the = sign; for example, jn=j. The result is shown in Figure 25.

Figure 26 illustrates a complex search for an article about leukemia in children in JAMA after 1996. Each search is done separately. Note the number of retrieved articles for each search: result numbers R1, R2, R3, and R4. Any result numbers can be combined. In this case, all of the results were combined. The final search, R5 (r1 and r2 and r3 and r4), retrieved five articles.

Figure 27 shows the formats available in the first two of five articles.

The object of a PowerTrac search is the same in all platforms: to generate a Result Set. As in EasyTrac, you can use Boolean operators AND, OR, and NOT to define relationships between terms. However, in PowerTrac, you can extend that to include Results Sets and terms; for example, R1 AND geriatrics NOT cancer.

Parentheses can be used to nest expressions in a PowerTrac search. In fact, you can nest them as deeply as you wish. Anything you place within parentheses will be executed first. The natural order of executing expressions after that is NOT, followed by AND, followed by OR.

Wildcards or truncation characters can be used to broaden your search in Subject Guide and Keyword searches.

• Use the asterisk (*) immediately after a word to search for articles with that word stem and any number of characters. For example, psych retrieves psychology, psychologist, psychiatry, psychiatrist, and psychotherapist.

• Use the question mark (?) to match an exact number of characters. For example, wom?n retrieves woman and women, gr?y retrieves gray and grey, Wh?te retrieves White and Whyte, r??1 retrieves reel, real, and rail.

• Use the exclamation point to match one or no characters. For example, color retrieves color or colour and favo!r retrieves favor or favour.

Range operators can be used to search for dates in the database publication list (see Table 3).

In short, PowerTrac gives you more options for narrowing your search. Generally, if your results total more than twenty citations, a limit search option displays. If the entire citation list includes twenty-one, twenty-two, or twenty-three citations, they will all be displayed.

Content Coverage

IAC takes a close look at content coverage. Current usage, competitive products, and customer feedback are evaluated to redefine products and add new sources. As a point of reference, over 200 full-text articles have been added to the Expanded Academic ASAP database in the last few months. This database alone includes approximately 1,600 index and 750 full-text articles. Additional articles are added as rights are received. The goal for that database is 1,000 full-text articles. It should be noted that there is a lag time of up to ninety days between the time that the articles are indexed and the time that full text is added and fiche numbers and film numbers are attached. Text files are updated daily. Full text is usually available four to six weeks after the original publication.

Article Selection Guidelines

Articles are indexed and abstracted to meet the objectives of each particular database. For example, Expanded Academic ASAP is designed to meet the needs of scholars, college students, and professionals in the social sciences, humanities, and hard sciences. The Health Reference Center databases cover diseases and pathological conditions, including their causes, diagnosis, and treatment, and health and wellness maintenance. Health Reference Center-Academic includes articles from health magazines and medical journals, pamphlets, reference books, and articles selected from approximately 3,000 general interest and business publications.

The minimum length of the articles selected in SearchBank is usually one-half of a page or about 400 words in standard-sized journals, one-quarter of a page in tabloid journals, or one-half of a column in newsletters. Minimum length of book reviews ranges from one-quarter to one-half of a page, unless they are from select journals such as Booklist, Choice, Horn Book, New York Times Book Review, Publishers Weekly, School Library Journal, Times Literary Supplement, or World Literature Today.

Articles for all databases must contain substantive information on a field of study. For example, the content criteria for Expanded Academic ASAP includes the following:

• Editorials about the contents of the issue; option articles

• Follow-up articles, commentary, and criticism

• Letters to the editors from authors who are prominent in their field of study

• Speeches, essays, and transcripts; obituaries and testimonies

• Product announcements and evaluations

• Statistical compilations

• Professional conferences or association meeting articles that discuss subject matter; sample problems and examination questions with answers

• Directories, bibliographies

• Articles with graphs, tables, and charts; addenda and notices of corrections; reviews (not abstracted)

• Short stories and novels excerpts, plays or poems (not abstracted).

In Health Reference Center--Academic, article selection tends to be more liberal from priority publications such as British Medical Journal, Lancet, JAMA, and the New England Journal of Medicine.

The number of titles and date ranges varies from database to database and from format to format. For example, see Table 5, which illustrates CD-ROM stand-alone and Internet coverage (character cell and Web access) in Expanded Academic ASAP.

Web System Requirements

The Remote Authentication Service allows your user population to access SearchBank through a Web browser twenty-four hours a day, seven days a week, so even when the library is closed, students can do their research. To make this available, (1) your library must be running a Web server that is configured to run a CGI script (sample scripts are available from IAC Distribution Systems) and (2) you must create a patron database or file that includes unique identifiers for each patron that can be checked to authenticate users. You can use a library-defined user name, password, or code, or a combination of all three. It is essential that someone update the file periodically to keep it current.

If you choose the InfoTrac SearchBank RPAS service to authenticate your users, it will match the entries with the unique identifier(s) in your patron database to determine whether there is a match and grant permission to the databases or deny it.

If you are contemplating using a service such as this, be prepared to volunteer the following information:

• What unique identifier(s) would you like your user population to enter to access the databases? It's a good idea to set guidelines and create a design if you are going to use a form.

• What is the URL that you will use to authenticate users? This is the http address of the server used to answer the requests sent by IAC to validate patrons.

• What is the TCP port number (e.g., port 80)?

• What is the path to the CGI script on your Web server that will process requests from IAC? If a user ID/password is required, this must also be provided.

Additional information can be obtained from Information Access Company by calling 1-800-227-8431 or e-mailing cemarketing@iacnet.com. See Table 6 for the full list of Customer Support options. You might also want to subscribe to one of InfoTrac's lists.

Conclusion

InfoTrac SearchBank's family of databases caters to the academic market. They provide a variety of sources on every platform used by libraries today. The dual mode interface allows students to choose from simple and advanced search modes. On the up side, (1) the Subject Guide seamlessly links users to subdivisions and related topics, or pertinent abstracts and articles; (2) Subject Guide is the default search mode-if the search term you enter does not match a subject in the controlled vocabulary, the Keyword search mode is initiated; and (3) PowerTrac allows you to do some very advanced set theory and field searching. The quality of the retrieved information is excellent. IAC is to be complimented for making information available in both English and Spanish. On the down side, there is no ability to limit by full text on the EasyTrac opening screen. We would like to see this option added, since most of the other databases we reviewed include it, and it is extremely useful. Additionally, we would like to see more current, full-text articles added to the family of databases.

The Web version is our choice, allowing students to research from remote locations twenty-four hours a day. SearchBank is definitely a reference center to evaluate.
